The Concept

I'm currently working on a 3D "friendslop" game called stardozing. In this game, you play as a little star-bellied catbear, which looks a little something like this.

We knew pretty early that we wanted them to be customizable, as we're hoping to support players in building a connection to their character and differentiate themselves from their friends, and set up a fun and simple way to reward player's efforts.

At some point, I had the idea that the stars on the catbears' bellies could be randomized somehow, and that they'd be tied to the Steam ID of each player. That way, they'd be completely personalized for each player--and better yet, it'd be really enticing; "you've got a special star design waiting for you and you won't know what it is until you give the game a try!"

The Process

Knowing I planned to use vertex displacement to make some funky star shapes, the first thing I did was hop into Blender and model some stars, making an object for four through eleven pointed stars.

Bringing them into Unity, I set up a little scene with a camera rendering to a render texture. Later on, I plan to make a copy of this texture on the actual catbear. For now, it just looks like we're about to listen to the Stone Temple Pilots.

Moving on, I set up a little shader that takes in an integer "seed" value, as well as two configurable parameters for the noise scale and offset, to displace each vertex of the star based on the seed input.

Next, I used the seed to randomly determine which star to activate. At this point, I realized that eight through eleven pointed stars look pretty awkward when randomly displaced, so I excluded them for now.
